# Music

I find it hard to listen to older recordings of me singing, and it's hard to look back and be "proud" of them. I think this is a pretty universal phenomenon for anyone who makes things - by the time you're capable of judging your own work properly, you've usually moved past whatever version of yourself made it. The taste that lets you spot the flaws only shows up after the fact.

That said, I think it's worth owning and respecting your past for what it is rather than hiding it away, so I wanted somewhere central to put these tracks up rather than letting them scatter across old platforms and get lost. It felt especially overdue after "Fluffy Little Dog" quietly picked up over 30 million streams and there was nowhere online that actually explained who wrote it.

Currently, I sing regularly as part of the One Voice community choir run by [The Grand](https://www.thegrand.org.uk/) and often post videos of my performances on my [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/spode) page.

I hope to release new tracks at some point, but I also don't want to ruin a good hobby by taking it too seriously.

Below you'll find links to (some) of my discography and I have made individual pages for each track so you can stream them, with lyrics and some explanation as to what was happening at the time.

## Badly Parked Sedona

![Badly Parked Sedona](badly-parked-sedona.jpeg)
*You'll be pleased to know, I never park like this.*

After Intrinsic, Richard Williams and I worked on quite a few side projects, but Badly Parked Sedona was one of the only projects that got as far as release.

I used to drive a Kia Sedona and as our first release (Kittens In the Snow) was intended as comedy - I thought a comedic sounding band name made sense.

Our songs ended up being more popular with children, so in hindsight the name was never really suitable.

## Intrinsic - The Cold Awakening

![Intrinsic](intrinsic-band-photo.jpg)
*It was hard to keep a straight face for this staged photo.*

Intrinsic was a band that started while I was at school, but we didn't start doing any original material until we all ended up at the same university (Kent at Canterbury).

I once overheard another band on campus describe us "pretentious w*nkers", and there was some truth to that. We all listened to way too much Dream Theater and a song without a lengthy guitar solo was never considered.

Looking back, this oblivious self-confidence had the side-effect of providing great motivation to just keep going, which can be difficult when surrounded by critics.

I was on lead vocals, with [Nick Eve](https://www.facebook.com/nickevemusic/?locale=en_GB) on Bass and backing vocals. Jon Clarke played Guitar and Richard Williams bounced between guitar and keyboard whilst also providing a lot of the initial songwriting. His brother, Neil Williams played the drums.

We mostly gigged around Canterbury, where we also won "Keynestock" the battle of the bands run by the university's Music Society and allowing us to warm up the crowd at the prom before The Bluetones came on. Granted, about 6 hours before they came on - but I'll take it.

We recorded and released this EP in 2003 using Adobe Audition on a machine that frequently crashed and a room padded with egg boxes. To say it was done on a shoe-string budget would be generous. However, the final result, is still very listenable.

My vocals used to get likened to Brian Molko a lot, as I had a habit over-twanging to the point of being nasal, a habit I have spent years struggling to undo. I do however, have a naturally bright voice - so technique was not entirely to blame.

As a band, I wanted us to focus on recording and Internet distribution rather than performing as I was convinced it was the way to go, but if memory serves - they wanted to focus on gigs instead. Not long after of course, MySpace happened and we all kicked ourselves.

When we released the album, I felt it was more important that people heard it than we made any money from it - so I did a short production run to sell to locals, and then I put all the MP3s on my website and just shared it out. 

I was a columnist for The Inquirer at the time and when I sent it to Mike Magee - he loved it and simply wrote a news piece on it sharing the link publicly, which I wasn't expecting. They used to get a lot of traffic, and I was well used to lost of people e-mailing me off the back of my columns, but I wasn't expecting the e-mails I got about the album. 

I remember distinctly one particular person messaging me, begging me to forget the tech and focus on the band - that we "had something special" and that you can always go back to computers in a decade. I'm not convinced he was right, but it was an inspiring message to read all the same (whoever he was).

One particularly memorable gig was for the 200th issue celebration for Computer Shopper magazine which is where this photo was taken. Off the back of that, NVIDIA asked us to perform for one of their developer conferences, which was nice to be asked for and I'm sure would have been a lot of fun - but by this point I had taken a step back from the band to focus on my journalism - the exact opposite to what had been asked of me in the e-mail.

The EP is somewhat eclectic - there's something for everyone I would say, but Dreamer was the most universally liked. 

After my own distribution, I released the album on [Jamendo](http://www.jamendo.com/album/28355) and then a few years back, Jon released it on [Apple Music, YouTube, Spotify, etc.](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=OLAK5uy_mboKfLbiQQjJWdvGhDJe6SwFacYi4-6Kc) using DiskoKid.
